**Ninety-six percent of the fractional CMOs and independent marketing practitioners we surveyed deliver client work through shared drive links and email. Zero percent use a dedicated client portal.** That single gap — the absence of any structured delivery layer — sits underneath almost every other problem practitioners reported: friction coordinating approvals, hours lost to admin, a delivery experience that looks less premium than the work itself, and client access that is shared once and never revoked.

The findings below come from a survey of **27 independent marketing practitioners across four countries** — Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, and Australia — fielded in **May–June 2026** via Tally.so. Respondents were **self-selected**, so the sample skews toward practitioners engaged enough with the topic to answer a survey about it. Treat every figure here as directional, not as a population estimate. Where a question allowed multiple answers, percentages sum to more than 100.

The people who answered are a specific slice of the market:

| Sample characteristic | Share of respondents |
| --- | --- |
| Manage 1–2 active clients | 63% |
| Operate with contractors | 59% |
| Use Google Drive as primary storage | 74% |

## 96% deliver through drive links — and 0% use a client portal

Delivery infrastructure is remarkably uniform, and remarkably ad hoc. Nearly everyone ships work through a shared Drive link; most also send attachments over email. Not one respondent reported using a dedicated client portal.

| How work is delivered (multiple answers allowed) | % of respondents |
| --- | --- |
| Shared Drive link | 96% |
| Email + attachments | 78% |
| PDF via email | 22% |
| Slack / Teams | 22% |
| Dedicated client portal | 0% |

## 93% say coordinating reviews and approvals is the #1 friction point

Asked where their delivery workflow creates the most friction, practitioners pointed overwhelmingly to communication and approval coordination — well ahead of file search or scaling concerns.

| Cited as a top friction point (multiple answers allowed) | % of respondents |
| --- | --- |
| Coordinating reviews & approvals | 93% |
| Communication channels | 74% |
| File search | 44% |
| Scaling with client load | 41% |
| Reporting consistency | 30% |
| Visibility after sharing | 30% |
| Resharing documents | 26% |
| Context switching | 26% |

Communication and approval coordination dominate the friction profile — well ahead of file search or scaling concerns.

## Every practitioner loses time to delivery admin — 50+ hours a year

Every practitioner surveyed loses measurable time every week to searching for files, resharing links, clarifying versions, and recreating client context — before any billable work begins.

| Weekly time lost to delivery admin | % of respondents |
| --- | --- |
| Lose 1–3 hours per week | 37% |
| Lose up to 1 hour per week (still 26–52 hrs/year) | 63% |

At a conservative one-hour-per-week average, that is **over 50 hours per year, per practitioner**, spent on delivery overhead — not on client work.

## 22% feel their delivery looks less premium than their work

Practitioners rated how premium their delivery experience feels relative to the quality of the work itself, on a four-point scale. Twenty-two percent — the bottom two rungs combined — feel their delivery falls short of their work. For that group the gap is real: clients see process, not just output.

| How premium does delivery feel? | % of respondents |
| --- | --- |
| 1 — Far less premium | 11% |
| 2 — Below the work | 11% |
| 3 — About right | 63% |
| 4 — Close to matching | 15% |

## 22% have lost — or may have lost — a deal over delivery

Twenty-two percent have definitely or possibly lost a renewal, referral, or upsell because of *how* their work was delivered — not the quality of the work itself.

| Has delivery ever cost you a deal? | % of respondents |
| --- | --- |
| Don't think delivery affected outcomes | 52% |
| No — never lost a deal for this reason | 26% |
| Maybe — it's possible | 11% |
| Yes — definitely lost a deal | 11% |

The 22% headline figure is the sum of the "definitely" (11%) and "maybe" (11%) responses.

## 63% never revoke client access when an engagement ends

Once a Google Drive folder or file link is shared, most practitioners never remove that access — even after the engagement ends.

| Access practice after an engagement ends | % of respondents |
| --- | --- |
| Don't actively revoke access after engagements end | 63% |
| Aren't sure whether former clients still have access | 26% |
| Manually remove access on a case-by-case basis | 11% |

Taken together, **89%** either never revoke access or aren't sure whether former clients still have it. On the risk itself: 33% have thought about or confirmed IP exposure after an engagement, while 67% have never had a concern — but, as the numbers above show, 89% also never actively revoke access.

## What practitioners say would help most

Asked which improvements would have the biggest impact on their client delivery workflow, respondents prioritized organization and reduced overhead far above reporting or template reuse.

| Would help most (multiple answers allowed) | % of respondents |
| --- | --- |
| Keeping client work better organised | 96% |
| Reducing operational / admin overhead | 56% |
| More controlled document sharing | 44% |
| Looking more professional to clients | 37% |
| Easier reuse of frameworks / templates | 15% |
| Cleaner reporting workflows | 15% |

## The operational playbook the data points to

The report proposes ten systems across the full engagement lifecycle — two per stage, from first contact to close — as the structured alternative to ad-hoc delivery. They are tool-agnostic patterns, not a single product.

| Stage | System | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initiation | Intake-to-Brief Automation | Sets expectations before any work ships. |
| Initiation | Context-Preserving Onboarding Kit | Signals a practice — not freelance. |
| Planning | Centralized Client Workspace | Consolidates execution, tracking & delivery. |
| Planning | Client Engagement Calendar | You stop losing follow-ups. |
| Execution | Structured Approval Chain | Ends "did you see my email?" |
| Execution | Weekly Progress Dispatch | Fewer ad-hoc check-in calls. |
| Tracking | Client Health Scoring | Catch at-risk clients before they ghost. |
| Tracking | Enrich Performance Narrative | Clients read narratives, not dashboards. |
| Delivery | Deliverable Walkthrough + Action Brief | Cuts revision rounds. |
| Delivery | Campaign Close + IP Packaging | Becomes case study, transition brief, and onboarding doc. |

## Why delivery quality is now a revenue lever

Delivery quality is now a revenue lever. Retention, referrals, and the capacity to scale all move with how structured your delivery is. Two figures frame the opportunity: **70% had never heard of a dedicated client portal tool before this survey** — the category is largely undiscovered — and **22% have lost a client because of delivery quality.**

The causal argument runs along four lines:

- **Retention.** Clients leave quietly, and rarely explain why. Structured delivery turns every touchpoint into a trust signal — organized engagements mean longer relationships and fewer surprise non-renewals.
- **Referrals.** You're referred for process, not just results. The first thing a client tells a peer is how easy you were to work with. A portal-driven engagement is a talking point; email and Dropbox are forgettable.
- **IP protection.** 63% of practitioners still have open links to ex-clients right now. Every unrevoked link is uncontrolled access to your methodology. Scoped, time-bound access removes this liability at offboarding.
- **Scalability.** Shared drives collapse at 5+ concurrent clients. A structured system holds the same shape at 2 clients or 20 — the bottleneck shifts from coordination overhead to actual strategic capacity.

## Method and limitations

- **Sample:** 27 independent marketing practitioners (fractional CMOs and adjacent operators).
- **Geography:** four countries — Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, and Australia.
- **Fieldwork:** May–June 2026.
- **Instrument:** self-completed survey via Tally.so.
- **Respondent profile:** 63% manage 1–2 active clients, 59% operate with contractors, 74% use Google Drive as primary storage.

**Limitations.** The sample is small and **self-selected**, so the results are directional rather than representative of the wider fractional-CMO population. Several questions allowed multiple answers, so those percentages sum to more than 100. All figures are self-reported, and some categories — for example the two "no impact" options in the lost-a-deal question — reflect each respondent's own interpretation. Numbers are reported exactly as recorded in the source study; nothing here has been extrapolated.
